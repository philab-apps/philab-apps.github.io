Effective date: November 11, 2026

Tongcap (the "app") never sends the images you capture, or anything in them, off your device. The developer receives only two kinds of information. Usage statistics, such as which features you used, are sent to Google's analytics service. They are on by default, and you can turn them off in Settings. In the EU, the EEA, the UK and Switzerland the app does not send usage statistics at all, and Settings has no such switch. And if you email the developer, we use your address and message to reply to you. This policy explains the information the app handles on your device, usage statistics, the information Apple handles, such as payments, and the emails you send us.

1. Information we process

The app has no sign-up and never asks, within the app, for your name, phone number, email, or other contact details. The developer runs no servers of its own.

What the app never sends off your device — even with usage statistics on

  • Captured images and their previews, and your edits (mosaics, annotations, crops)
  • Addresses and titles of captured pages, and names you give items. The full address and the names are kept only on your device, in the library's list file; on screen, an item shows the name you gave it or, if you haven't named it, only the domain. Deleting the item deletes them too
  • Names you give library folders. They're kept only on your device, in the library's folder list file; deleting a folder deletes its name too
  • The positions of faces found in images, and text such as amounts, phone numbers, account numbers, email addresses, and Korean resident registration numbers
  • The editing state you leave behind with "Save" in the editor. Only the kind and position of each hidden box, whether it is hidden, the annotations, and the crop and split settings are kept in a file beside the item. Recognized text is never saved. Deleting the item deletes them too
  • The screen recording you choose in Photos. A copy stays in the app only while stitching, and is deleted once stitching is done. So that you can choose to delete the original recording right after stitching, the app keeps the identifier Photos gave that video in memory only, and only while the result screen is open. The identifier is never saved or sent, and it is discarded when you close the result screen or the deletion ends
  • Website data, such as cookies and caches, from sites opened in the in-app browser (included in device backups, Section 3)
  • Your settings (included in device backups, Section 3)

The in-app browser doesn't keep a list of the sites you visit, and when you close it, the back list goes with it. Cookies and caches (Section 9), however, do keep traces of the sites you visited.

Usage statistics (on by default; you can turn them off in Settings)

Settings › Privacy › "Share Usage Statistics" is on from the start, so for as long as it stays on, the app sends the following to Google Analytics for Firebase (Section 5). The first screen you see when you open the app for the first time tells you this once, and where to turn it off. In the EU, the EEA, the UK and Switzerland the app does not turn usage statistics on, sends nothing, and the switch does not appear in Settings (Section 2). If you turn it off, nothing is sent from then on, and it stays off even when you reopen the app.

  • What happens in the app: a capture starting, finishing or failing, exporting with a free trial, the purchase screen appearing and closing, the result of tapping a purchase or restore button (completed, cancelled, waiting for approval, failed, product details could not be loaded, restored, or nothing to restore), an export finishing (whether it was a web capture or a screen recording, whether it was saved to Photos, shared or copied, the file format, whether mosaics, crops, annotations, splitting or stitching were used, and whether you have purchased), and how many captures and folders are in the library when you open the app. The only values attached are fixed kinds and ranges (for example "web capture", "4–10 screens tall", "5–15 seconds", the failure reason "network", or "6–20 captures"). For edits we attach only whether they were used, never what was hidden or how many. No price, currency, product ID or transaction ID is attached to a purchase or restore result
  • Records Google's analytics tool keeps on its own: when you first opened the app with usage statistics on, when you open the app and how long you use it, and app and iOS updates. If you make an App Store purchase, the tool may also record the purchase on its own, including the product ID, price, currency and quantity
  • Device information: device type, iOS version, app version, and language setting
  • Two random numbers: the app instance ID groups records from the same installation in the statistics, and the Firebase installation ID lets Google tell this app's installations apart. Both are created when the app is first used with usage statistics on, and are not linked to your name, contact details or Apple Account. When you turn usage statistics off, nothing is sent and both stay on your device until you turn usage statistics on again, when the app instance ID is deleted and a new one is created. The installation ID is not reused if you delete the app, and Google says it deletes an installation ID if the app is not used for 270 days (Section 5)
  • Internet address (IP address) and approximate region (country and state or province): when you connect, Google receives your internet address and estimates the region from it. Google says its analytics tool does not log or store the internet address (Section 5). The app does not use Location Services

The app does not collect the advertising identifier (IDFA) and does not track your activity across other apps or websites. Usage statistics are never used for advertising or combined with other companies' data.

Handled by Apple

  • Payments are processed by the App Store. The app never receives payment details such as card numbers; it only checks on your device whether you have purchased
  • If you turn on "Share with App Developers" in your device settings, Apple collects crash reports and usage and gives them to the developer in a form that doesn't identify you. You can turn this off in Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements. It is separate from the app's "Share Usage Statistics"
  • If you use iCloud on your device, the number of free trials used (a single number) is stored in iCloud under your Apple Account and kept in sync with your other devices that use the same account. Nothing such as your name or device information is stored with it, and the developer can't see this value. It stays in iCloud even after you delete the app (Section 3). While you aren't using iCloud, it stays only on your device

What happens outside the app

  • Websites you open in the app follow their own privacy policies. The app does not decide what those sites collect
  • If you tap "Privacy Policy" in Settings while connected to the internet, the app opens the web version of this policy in an in-app Safari view. Notion hosts that page, so the moment you open it, Notion receives connection information such as your internet address (IP) and cookies. The app and the developer do not receive this information, and the app does not send your captures, edits, or settings along with it. If you are not connected to the internet, the app shows the copy of this policy stored in the app
  • "Capture Full Page with Tongcap" in the share sheet receives only the address of the page you shared. It does not receive the original browser's sign-in state or cookies, and it keeps no cookies or caches from the page it opens to capture
  • "There's a link on your clipboard" on the home screen only checks whether the clipboard holds something shaped like an address; it does not read the contents. Pasting happens only when you tap the button

Emails you send us

  • If you email the developer (Section 10), we receive your email address, your name if your email shows it, whatever you write or attach, and information that travels with the email, such as when it was sent. We receive these emails with Gmail, an email service of Google LLC, and they are stored on Google's servers outside Korea (Section 5)

2. Why we process it

Information on your device is used for capturing, editing, exporting, and your library. It is also used to check whether you have purchased and to keep count of your free trials. It is never used for advertising or marketing.

The identifier Photos gave a video is used only to find that one video when you delete the original recording. It is used only when you tap delete in the "Original Recording" row on the result screen; the identifier is kept in memory only and is never saved or sent.

Usage statistics are used only to fix and improve the app: to see where people get stuck, which ways of capturing fail, which features and editing tools are used, and how the free trials and the purchase screen lead to purchases. They are never used for advertising or marketing, and we don't try to find out whose records they are. Which legal basis applies depends on where you are. The app tells this from your App Store country (the country of your App Store account, which a VPN or travel does not change). If it cannot read it, it uses your device's region setting.

  • EU, EEA, United Kingdom and Switzerland: no usage statistics, so no legal basis (not processed). The app does not start Google's analytics tool there, so nothing is sent and no random numbers are created. The app does not ask you, and Settings › Privacy does not show the "Share Usage Statistics" switch.
  • Everywhere else: our legitimate interest in fixing and improving the app (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)), and we do not ask for your consent. We weigh it against your rights as follows. We use only random numbers and fixed values that are not linked to your name, contact details or Apple Account, and we never send your captures or the addresses, titles or text in them. We do not use the data for advertising or combine it with other companies' data. Records are deleted 14 months after they are received. And you can object at any time by turning the setting off, without losing any feature of the app (Section 7).

The address and contents of an email you send us are used only to reply to it. The legal basis for this is our legitimate interest in answering your questions (GDPR Article 6(1)(f)) or, if your question is about a purchase, performing our contract with you (GDPR Article 6(1)(b)). We use Gmail to receive and keep these emails, relying on our legitimate interest in using a dependable email service (Article 6(1)(f) of the GDPR and the UK GDPR). Section 5 describes what Google does with them.

3. How long we keep it

Information on your device is deleted as soon as you remove it from the library, and everything is deleted when you delete the app.

Four things remain even after you delete the app.

  • Your library is not included in device backups. This keeps your captures from leaving the device through a backup. It will not follow you to a new phone, so save anything you want to keep to Photos. Settings, the free trial count, and website data are included in backups
  • Copies saved to Photos or exported to other apps stay in those apps
  • An image copied to the clipboard may be passed to your other devices using the same Apple Account. Turning off Handoff in your device settings stops this
  • The number of free trials used, stored in iCloud (Section 1). You can manage it in your device's iCloud settings

Usage statistics are deleted automatically, record by record, 14 months after Google receives them. Only totals that combine many people's records, such as the number of users per day, remain after that. When you turn usage statistics off, nothing is sent from then on. The app instance ID and any records not yet sent stay on your device, unsent, until you turn usage statistics on again, when they are deleted before anything is sent; deleting the app deletes them too. Google says it deletes an installation ID if the app is not used for 270 days. Deleting an installation ID does not change how long records already sent are kept (14 months, above).

Emails you send the developer are deleted once a year has passed since our last exchange (or, for an email we didn't reply to, since we received it), by the end of the following month at the latest. If we learn that an email came from a child under 13, we don't keep it for a year: we delete it, together with our reply, right after we reply, or as soon as we learn this if we learn it only after replying or don't reply. If you want yours deleted sooner, ask at the same address (Section 10).

4. Sharing with third parties

We do not share personal information with third parties. Google processes usage statistics on our behalf as our service provider, and Gmail (Google) receives the emails you send us; both are described in Section 5.

5. Service providers and transfers abroad

We have Google analyze usage statistics on our behalf, and so usage statistics are transferred abroad. The emails you send us are also stored outside Korea, as described below. In addition, the free trial count in Section 1 is stored in your iCloud (Apple).

Usage statistics (Google Analytics for Firebase)

We have accepted Google's data processing terms (Google Ads Data Processing Terms, business.safety.google/adsprocessorterms), under which Google processes usage statistics only on our instructions. All data sharing settings that would let Google use this data for its own services are turned off.

  • Service provider and recipient: Google LLC, 1600 Amphitheatre Parkway, Mountain View, CA 94043, USA
  • What it does: collects, stores and totals usage statistics, and prepares the statistics reports the developer sees
  • Countries: the United States and other countries where Google operates data centers. Google says it may process data in any country where it has facilities; the countries it has published are the same as for emails below
  • What is transferred: the "Usage statistics" items in Section 1
  • Internet address: Google receives it when you connect. Google says Google Analytics does not log or store internet addresses and uses them to protect the service and to estimate the approximate region (support.google.com/analytics/answer/6004245)
  • When and how: from time to time while usage statistics are on and you use the app, encrypted, over the internet
  • Purpose and retention: used only to prepare statistics reports for the developer. Individual records are deleted 14 months after they are received, and totals that combine many people's records are kept. If we end our agreement with Google, Google's terms say it deletes the remaining data within 180 days at most. Google says it deletes an installation ID if the app is not used for 270 days (firebase.google.com/docs/projects/manage-installations); this does not change how long records already sent are kept
  • Safeguards: Google's data processing terms include standard contractual clauses for transfers. The app's code allows only fixed values to be sent (Section 8), and data is sent only over encrypted connections. In the EU, the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, nothing is sent (Section 2)
  • If you don't want this transfer: turn off Settings › Privacy › "Share Usage Statistics"; nothing at all is sent from then on. It is on by default, and the first screen you see when you open the app tells you this and where to turn it off. Leaving it off doesn't limit any feature of the app

Emails you send us (Gmail)

We receive your emails with Gmail, Google's free email service for personal use, and Google stores them on its servers outside Korea. For Gmail, we have no separate data processing agreement with Google. Google provides Gmail under its own Terms of Service and Privacy Policy (policies.google.com/privacy), and also handles these emails for its own purposes, such as keeping the service secure and filtering spam.

  • Recipient: Google LLC (Gmail), 1600 Amphitheatre Parkway, Mountain View, CA 94043, USA
  • What it does: receives, stores and delivers the emails you send us, and checks them for spam and malware
  • Countries: Google says it operates data centers in the United States, Chile, Uruguay, Ireland, the United Kingdom, the Netherlands, Belgium, Denmark, Finland, Germany, Japan, Taiwan and Singapore (as of October 2026, datacenters.google/locations). A personal Gmail account can't choose where emails are stored, so we can't tell which of these countries holds a given email. While your emails are stored in another country, the courts, law enforcement and national security authorities of that country may be able to access them under its laws
  • What is transferred: the email details in Section 1 (your email address, your name, what you write or attach, when it was sent, and so on)
  • When and how: over the internet, at the moment you send the email
  • Purpose and retention: Google uses the emails to store and deliver them and to run and improve Gmail, for example by detecting spam, malware and abuse. They stay until we delete them, which we do as described in Section 3. According to Google, once we delete an email it is usually removed from Google's active systems within about 2 months and from its backups within up to 6 months, although Google may keep some information longer for reasons such as security or legal obligations (policies.google.com/technologies/retention)
  • Safeguards: Google LLC states that it participates in the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, its UK Extension and the Swiss-U.S. Data Privacy Framework (policies.google.com/privacy/frameworks). We also protect the mailbox as described in Section 8
  • If you don't want this transfer: don't email us. There is currently no other way to contact us. Not emailing us doesn't limit your use of the app in any way

6. Deletion

Information on your device is deleted from the device when you remove it from the library or delete the app. The only information kept off your device is usage statistics at Google and the emails you send the developer, each deleted after the period in Section 3.

The app deletes the following on its own.

  • Temporary files used for capturing and exporting: deleted when the task finishes. If the app was shut down mid-task, they are deleted the next time it starts, before any screen appears
  • The app's copy of the screen recording you chose: deleted right after stitching finishes or fails
  • Results captured through the share sheet: deleted as the app moves them into the library
  • An item's editing state and preview tiles: deleted together with that item
  • The full-resolution tiles used for zooming in: deleted together with the item. They sit in the device's cache folder, so the system may delete them first when storage runs low
  • The app instance ID and records not yet sent for usage statistics: when you turn usage statistics off, nothing is sent and they stay on the device only; they are deleted before anything is sent when you turn usage statistics on again. Deleting the app deletes them too

The app does not delete the original recording in Photos by default. Only if, right after a capture from a recording is made, you tap delete in the "Original Recording" row on the result screen and allow iOS's own deletion prompt, the one recording you just chose is moved to the Recently Deleted album in Photos, where it stays for 30 days before Photos deletes it permanently. This is Photos' own rule; if your iCloud storage is full, for example, it may be removed right away and cannot be restored. If you use iCloud Photos, it is deleted on your other devices signed in with the same Apple Account as well. The app does not save the identifier, so once you close the result screen it can no longer delete that recording, and you will need to delete it in Photos yourself.

Deletion means deleting files on the device. Google deletes usage statistics automatically according to the retention setting (14 months). For emails, we delete both your email and our reply from Inbox, Sent and All Mail, then empty the trash so they are permanently deleted. According to Google, it then usually takes about 2 months for them to be fully removed from Google's servers and up to 6 months from its backups, and some information may be kept longer for reasons such as security or legal obligations (Section 5). During that time, we don't restore or use the deleted emails. Nothing is printed on paper. The app cannot delete the four things in Section 3 (device backups, exported copies, clipboard images passed to another device, and the free trial count stored in iCloud).

7. Your rights and responsibilities

At any time, you may ask to access, correct, or delete your personal information, to restrict its processing, or to receive it in a portable format, and you may object to its processing. Everything the app handles, except the free trial count stored in iCloud (Section 1) and usage statistics, stays on your device, so you can open it, edit it, and delete it yourself in the library. Deleting the app removes all of it.

To stop or object to usage statistics, turn off Settings › Privacy › "Share Usage Statistics". From then on nothing at all is sent, and it stays off even when you reopen the app. The app instance ID and any records not yet sent are deleted on your device when you turn usage statistics on again, before anything is sent; to delete them right away, delete the app. Records already sent carry no name or contact details, so we can't tell which records are yours and can't look them up or delete them individually; they are deleted after the period in Section 3.

Emails you send the developer are kept by the developer. To exercise any of these rights over them, write to the contact in Section 10. If you are in the EU, the EEA, the UK or Switzerland, you also have the right to lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority (Section 11). For what Google does with your emails for its own purposes (Section 5), you can also contact Google as described in its Privacy Policy.

The four things in Section 3 are outside the app. Delete them in your device's backup settings, in the app you saved them to, on the clipboard of the device that received them, and in your device's iCloud settings.

A parent or legal guardian may exercise these rights on a child's behalf. The app has no sign-up, so it does not know a user's age, and it does not collect children's information separately. The way to exercise them is the same: delete the library items on the child's device, or delete the app. The app is not directed at children under 13, but usage statistics are on by default, so a parent or guardian of a child under 13 should turn off Settings › Privacy › "Share Usage Statistics" on the child's device. If you believe a child under 13 has sent us personal information, for example by email, contact us at the address in Section 10 and we will delete it. If we learn this on our own, we delete it as described in Section 3.

For any other questions, use the contact details in Section 10.

Your captures may include other people's personal information. Please hide that information before you share a capture.

8. Safeguards

  • Capturing, editing, face position detection, and text recognition all happen on your device
  • Faces are only located. The app does not identify who they are, and never creates or stores facial features (biometric data). A saved edit keeps only the position of each hidden box
  • Saving the images you make to Photos uses add-only access. It is asked for the first time you save, used only for saving, and never to read your photo library
  • Full Access to Photos is asked for once, and only when you tap delete in the "Original Recording" row on the result screen right after a capture from a recording. If you don't tap it, this permission is never requested, and by default nothing is deleted. Even if you allow it, the app looks up only the one recording you just chose and asks to delete it (iOS asks you to confirm again before deleting), and does not scan or read any other photo or video
  • You can reduce or remove the Photos access you gave at any time in Settings › Apps › Tongcap › Photos. The share sheet extension uses only add-only access
  • Only the one screen recording you choose in the photo picker comes into the app. The app does not search through your photo library
  • Library files are marked with a protection level that keeps them from being opened while the device is locked
  • The preview left in the app switcher when the app is in the background hides the result and editor screens
  • The app's code allows usage statistics to carry only fixed kinds and ranges of values. There is no place in them for addresses, titles, file names or anything you type
  • Nothing is sent for people who have turned usage statistics off. The advertising identifier (IDFA) and the identifier for vendors (IDFV) are not sent, and ad storage and ad personalization signals are turned off. If you turn usage statistics off after using the app with them on, collection stops and nothing is sent from then on
  • Settings on Google's side: Google signals off, no links to advertising services, all data sharing settings off, collection of granular location and device data (such as city and device model) off, and data retention of 14 months, not extended by new activity
  • The mailbox for your emails is a dedicated account used only by the developer, with 2-Step Verification turned on using a security key or passkey
  • Gmail is set not to save senders' addresses to contacts automatically, and Gmail's smart features, which use email content, are turned off
  • We read your emails only in Gmail on the web or in the Gmail app, don't download them to other email apps or devices, and don't move or forward them anywhere else
  • Only the minimum information needed is processed

9. Automatic collection

While usage statistics are on, Google Analytics for Firebase in the app stores random numbers (app instance ID and installation ID) on your device and automatically collects and sends records of your use of the app (Sections 1 and 5). It is on by default (in the EU, the EEA, the UK and Switzerland it is never started, Section 2), so elsewhere it starts working when you begin using the app. To refuse it, turn off Settings › Privacy › "Share Usage Statistics". Turning it off stops everything from being sent from then on, and it stays off even when you reopen the app. The app instance ID and unsent records stored on your device are deleted when you turn it on again, and deleting the app deletes them too. The installation ID is not deleted when you turn it on again, and it is not reused if you delete the app (Section 1).

Websites you open in the in-app browser may leave cookies. Website data such as cookies and caches is stored on your device to keep you signed in, and the app does not read it or send it anywhere.

This data stays until you clear it. You can clear it any time with "Clear Web Logins" in Settings. Otherwise, to refuse it, don’t open that site in the in-app browser, use the opt-out the site offers, or delete the app.

Do Not Track. The app does not collect information about your activity across other websites or online services, so there is nothing for it to change in response to a browser's Do Not Track signal. Usage statistics cover only your use of this app, and only if you turn them on. Websites you open in the in-app browser, and third parties on those sites, may collect that kind of information; how they respond to Do Not Track is up to them.

11. Remedies

For advice or to make a complaint, you can contact the privacy authority where you live. For example:

  • EU or EEA: the data protection supervisory authority of the country where you live or work
  • United Kingdom: the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO), ico.org.uk
  • Switzerland: the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner (FDPIC), edoeb.admin.ch
  • United States: the Federal Trade Commission (FTC), reportfraud.ftc.gov

Because the developer is based in Korea, you can also contact the following Korean authorities.

  • Personal Information Infringement Report Center (operated by KISA): privacy.kisa.or.kr · 118 (in Korea)
  • Personal Information Dispute Mediation Committee: www.kopico.go.kr · 1833-6972
  • Cybercrime Investigation Division, Supreme Prosecutor's Office: www.spo.go.kr · 1301 (in Korea)
  • Cyber Security Division, National Police Agency: ecrm.police.go.kr · 182 (in Korea)

12. Changes

If this policy changes, we first update the policy at its public web address, then include the change in the next app update. The changes and the new effective date are noted at the top of the policy, and the changes apply from that date. If what usage statistics send, why, who receives them, how long they are kept, or how to turn them off changes, we also say so in the app's update notes. For a major change, such as new kinds of data being sent or a different recipient, we tell you inside the app before the new data is sent.
